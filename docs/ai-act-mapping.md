# EU AI Act and GDPR mapping

upbox is a **deployer-side** technical measure. It does not certify
you compliant. It produces evidence and controls that compliance
assessments ask for.

## Read this first: the dates moved

[Regulation (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj),
the Digital Omnibus on AI, was adopted on 8 July 2026 and published in
the Official Journal on 24 July 2026. It amended Article 113 of the AI
Act and deferred **Chapter III, Sections 1, 2 and 3**:

| High-risk category | Original date | New date |
|---|---|---|
| Article 6(2) / Annex III (stand-alone) | 2 August 2026 | **2 December 2027** |
| Article 6(1) / Annex I (embedded in regulated products) | 2 August 2027 | **2 August 2028** |

**Article 26 sits in Chapter III, Section 3.** The deployer obligations,
including the Article 26(6) log-retention duty, are therefore *not in
application* as of August 2026.

What did start on **2 August 2026**: the Article 50 transparency
obligations, the innovation-support measures, and enforcement at
national and EU level. Prohibitions added by the Omnibus covering
non-consensual intimate imagery apply from 2 December 2026.

Earlier versions of this document claimed "full obligations for
high-risk systems take effect 2 August 2026." That was correct when
written and became wrong on 24 July 2026. It is corrected here.

## Is your organisation even a high-risk deployer?

Usually not, for the tools upbox watches. Annex III covers employment
and worker management, education, credit scoring, biometrics, essential
services, and law enforcement. A developer running Cursor, Claude Code,
or Copilot to write software is not, by that act, deploying an Annex III
system.

Treat Article 26 as a **December 2027 runway** that matters if your
organisation deploys a high-risk system somewhere (HR screening is the
common one), not as a live August 2026 obligation for your IDE.

## Is upbox itself an AI system?

No. upbox is deterministic pattern matching over network traffic, with
rules written by people. Recital 12 and the Commission's guidelines on
the definition of an AI system exclude systems of this kind. upbox is a
governance tool, not a regulated AI system.

## AI Act articles

### Article 4: AI literacy (in force since 2 February 2025, amended 2026)

The Digital Omnibus replaced the original text. Providers and deployers
must "take measures to support the development of AI literacy" of their
staff, and the amended article expressly states that this does not
require guaranteeing any specific level of AI literacy.

upbox supplies artefacts rather than slides:

- Dashboard shows live traffic by tool, host, request size.
- Audit log is queryable and exportable.
- Per-tool allowlist enforces destination policy.

### Article 50: transparency (applies 2 August 2026)

This is the AI Act obligation that actually landed in August 2026.
upbox records every observed AI-system interaction, so deployers can
evidence what was sent, when, where, by which tool, at what size, and
with what outcome.

### Article 26: deployer obligations (from 2 December 2027)

When the obligation arrives, the audit log supports Article 26(1)
(use in accordance with instructions) and Article 26(5) (monitoring
operation, reporting risks).

**One honest limitation.** Article 26(6) requires deployers to keep
"logs automatically generated by that high-risk AI system to the extent
such logs are under their control," for at least six months. Those are
the provider-designed logs contemplated by Article 12, emitted by the
system itself. upbox observes the network from outside the system. Its
records corroborate those logs and are useful evidence in their own
right, but they are not the Article 26(6) logs and upbox cannot make
them so.

Article 26(7) also requires employers deploying a high-risk system at
the workplace to inform workers' representatives and affected workers
beforehand. See the workplace-monitoring warning below, which binds
today regardless of the AI Act timeline.

The audit log table (`requests` in `~/.upbox/upbox.db`) records:

| Field | Relevance |
|---|---|
| `ts` | Timestamp of every interaction (capture-host wall clock, not attested) |
| `tool` | Which AI tool was fingerprinted |
| `method`, `scheme`, `host`, `path` | Destination of the call |
| `req_bytes`, `resp_bytes`, `status` | Size and outcome |
| `headers_json` | Request headers, with auth-bearing headers redacted |
| `body_excerpt` | First 100 KB of the request body |
| `body_hash` | SHA-256 of the full body as forwarded, post-redaction |
| `redactions_applied_json` | Which redaction rules fired |
| `enforcement` | `NULL` = on-allowlist, `flagged` = off-allowlist but forwarded, `blocked` = stopped with a 403 |

`flagged` requests reached the cloud. Only `blocked` ones did not.

Export with `upbox export --format audit|jsonl|csv [--since TS --until TS --tool NAME]`.

`audit` writes `upbox.audit.v1`, which carries the ruleset digests, the
hash-chain verification result, what retention removed, and a coverage
statement, so the file can be handed to an auditor without a covering note.
`jsonl` and `csv` are flat dumps for spreadsheets and scripts.

### Article 99: penalties

The penalties chapter has applied since 2 August 2025. The Digital
Omnibus adjusted the enforcement regime, including SME-capped fines and
non-monetary measures, and added AI Office investigative powers. upbox
helps demonstrate concrete technical measures taken in good faith.

## GDPR articles

These apply now, and are the stronger reason to run upbox in 2026.

### Article 5: principles (data minimisation, storage limitation)

The redaction engine strips secrets and PII **before** the request
reaches the cloud provider, so the body the provider sees is already
minimised. Retention policy addresses Article 5(1)(e) storage
limitation.

### Article 30: records of processing

Per-tool destination and volume data, exportable to CSV, feeds the
record of processing activities.

### Article 15: right of access

`upbox report` produces, for the machine's user, the categories of data
processed and whether each is stored, the recipients (destination hosts per
tool), the retention period in force, the source (network observation on this
machine), and how to obtain a copy (`--records`, or `upbox export --format
audit`) and request erasure. The controller's identity is the deployer's to
fill in; upbox states that explicitly rather than guessing. Bodies may contain
personal data of third parties that upbox cannot separate from the user's own;
the report says so.

### Article 17: right to erasure

`upbox erase` removes individual records on request. Because the audit log is a
hash chain, a row cannot simply vanish without the log reading as tampered
with; instead it becomes a tombstone that keeps only its timestamp, sequence
number, and hashes. Every content column, including the body and header digests,
is cleared. The erasure, its time, and its reason are disclosed by `upbox
verify`, in every `upbox.audit.v1` export, and in the transparency report. A
legal hold (`upbox hold`) refuses erasure until it is released, which is the
Article 17(3)(e) case of data needed for legal claims.

### Article 32: security of processing

Visibility and control over data leaving the endpoint. Local-only CA
the user installs and can uninstall. Dashboard binds `127.0.0.1` only.

**At rest:** upbox does not encrypt its own database. It enforces
restrictive file permissions and relies on full-disk encryption
(FileVault, BitLocker, LUKS). See the "At rest" section of the README
for why application-level encryption was rejected.

### Article 35: DPIA

Concrete per-tool data flows feed DPIA templates:

| Tool | Destinations | Data classes seen | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cursor | api.cursor.sh, api2.cursor.sh | prompt text, file content | from audit log |
| Claude Code | api.anthropic.com | prompt text | from audit log |
| (etc.) | | | |

`upbox export --format csv` produces a spreadsheet feedable to a DPIA
process.

### Article 88: processing in the employment context

**Read this before deploying upbox on anyone else's machine.**

upbox works by installing a local CA and intercepting TLS. On an
employee device that is employee monitoring, and Article 88 leaves the
rules largely to national law. In several Member States the constraints
are strict and predate the AI Act. In Italy, for example, software that
enables remote monitoring of employees requires a union agreement or
Labour Inspectorate authorisation under Article 4 of Law 300/1970, and
the Garante has acted against deployments that lacked one.

Practical measures upbox ships or recommends:

- The curated capture-process default limits interception to AI tools
  rather than all traffic.
- The TLS exclusion list keeps banking, health, webmail, and government
  destinations tunnelled without decryption.
- Inform workers and their representatives before deployment, and
  document the legal basis.

Self-hosting upbox on your own machine raises none of this. Deploying
it across a workforce does.
